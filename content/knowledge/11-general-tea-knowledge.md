# General Tea Knowledge

This file provides general, brand-independent tea knowledge so the assistant can answer
broader tea questions (brewing, caffeine, health, storage) that customers may ask
alongside Ultra Rich specific questions. When relevant, connect the answer back to
Ultra Rich (e.g., its Kenyan black tea / CTC style), but general facts below are not
Ultra Rich specific claims unless stated.

## Black tea vs. green tea

- **Black tea** is fully oxidized during processing, giving it a darker color, bolder
  flavor, and generally higher caffeine content than green tea. It's the base of typical
  South Asian "chai"/milk tea.
- **Green tea** is minimally oxidized, giving a lighter color, grassier/more delicate
  flavor, and generally lower caffeine than black tea. Ultra Rich's green tea bags come
  in mint and lemon variants.
- Both come from the same plant, *Camellia sinensis*; the difference is processing, not
  a different plant.

## CTC vs. orthodox processing

- **CTC (Crush-Tear-Curl)** processing produces small, dense granules that infuse
  quickly and give a strong, brisk cup — well suited to milk tea, and typical of many
  Kenyan and South Asian black teas, including Ultra Rich.
- **Orthodox** processing keeps leaves more whole/twisted, generally brewing a more
  delicate, nuanced cup, often preferred for tea drunk without milk.

## Brewing black tea for milk tea ("doodh patti" style)

A general approach (not an official Ultra Rich recipe, just standard guidance):
1. Bring water to a boil.
2. Add tea leaves (loose or from a tea bag) and let it boil for a few minutes to develop
   color and strength.
3. Add milk and simmer gently rather than boiling hard, to avoid a scorched taste.
4. Add sugar to taste.
5. Strain and serve hot.

Brewing longer / using more leaf produces a darker, stronger cup (see the "Black Tea
Shades" guide for how color maps to strength and flavor).

## Caffeine content (general guidance)

Black tea typically contains roughly 40–70mg of caffeine per 240ml (8oz) cup, depending
on strength and brew time — generally less than coffee (which is often 95mg+ per cup)
but more than green tea (usually 20–45mg per cup). Brewing stronger/longer increases
caffeine extraction. If a customer asks for an exact caffeine number for a specific
Ultra Rich product, be honest that this isn't tested/published in the available
information, and give the general range above rather than inventing a precise figure.

## Storage

- Store tea in an airtight container, away from direct sunlight, moisture, and strong
  odors (tea readily absorbs surrounding smells).
- A cool, dry pantry or cupboard is better than storing near a stove or in a humid
  kitchen area.
- Properly sealed black tea generally keeps its quality well for many months to a
  couple of years; it doesn't "expire" suddenly but gradually loses aroma/freshness over
  time, which is part of why Ultra Rich emphasizes its aseptic freshness seal.

## Health-related questions

For any health, medical, pregnancy, or medication-interaction question about tea or
caffeine, give general, widely known information only (e.g., "moderate tea consumption
is generally considered safe for most healthy adults; pregnant people and those
sensitive to caffeine are often advised to moderate intake") and clearly recommend
consulting a doctor for anything specific to their situation. Don't give personalized
medical advice.
