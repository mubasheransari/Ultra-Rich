# Black Tea Shades — How Tea Color Reveals Flavor

Ultra Rich uses a five-shade guide to help people understand how the color of brewed
black tea relates to its strength and flavor notes. This is a simplified tasting guide,
not five different products — it describes the spectrum you get by brewing the same tea
lighter or stronger (or by comparing different Ultra Rich formats/brew times).

| Shade | Strength | Flavor notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light Golden Amber | Light / Bright | Honey, peach, orange zest, light malt |
| Copper Orange | Balanced | Toffee, apricot, biscuit, raisin |
| Rich Red-Orange | Classic Bold | Blackberry, caramel, almond |
| Deep Ruby Red | Robust | Plum, dark chocolate, roasted nuts |
| Mahogany Brown | Intense / Strong | Leather, oak, molasses, clove |

## How to read this as a customer

- If you like a **light, bright** cup (common for a first cup of the day with less
  brewing time), you'll land near **Light Golden Amber** or **Copper Orange**.
- If you like a **classic, balanced "doodh patti"-style** cup, **Rich Red-Orange** is the
  usual sweet spot — bold but not bitter.
- If you like your tea **very strong / stewed longer**, you'll get **Deep Ruby Red** to
  **Mahogany Brown**, with deeper, more intense notes (dark chocolate, molasses, clove).

## Practical brewing takeaway

Darker shade generally comes from a longer steep / boil time and more leaf relative to
water, not from a different tea. If a customer says their cup is "too light," the
practical advice is: use a bit more tea (or CTC leaf), or let it boil a little longer
before adding milk (for milk tea), rather than assuming the product itself is weak.
